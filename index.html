Paris en famille

Thursday 12 & Friday 13 November 2026 · from our place in the 12e · Metro, bus & on foot

Weather

Dry both days, but cold: Thursday about 2 → 10 °C, and Friday starts below freezing (−1 °C) and reaches about 10 °C. Bring hats, gloves and a stroller rain/wind cover. It gets dark about 17:15.

Getting around

  • Your stations: Picpus and Bel-Air (line 6) are a few minutes' walk away; Nation (lines 1, 2, 6, 9, RER A) is a bit further and has a direct line 1 to the Louvre.
  • Line 6 is the fun one: it runs straight from your area to the Eiffel Tower (Bir-Hakeim/Trocadéro), partly above ground, and crosses the Seine right next to the tower.
  • Stroller: most Metro stations have stairs and no lifts. Buses are step-free, so with a sleeping toddler pick the bus option in Google Maps.
  • Tickets: children under 4 ride free; your 4½-year-old needs a reduced (child) fare. Buy tickets in the official Île-de-France Mobilités / Bonjour RATP app or at station machines.
  • Status: as of 10 Nov no strike was announced for these days. Line 7bis is closed all week, but you don't need it. Check the Bonjour RATP app each morning.

Book ahead

  • Notre-Dame: free, timed slot on notredamedeparis.fr (opens 2–3 days before). Walk-ins are usually fine in November.
  • Le Procope (Thu lunch), Le Picotin (Thu dinner), Café Marly (Fri lunch), Le Square Trousseau (Fri dinner): tap "Map" for each place's phone and booking link. Ask for a high chair and room for a stroller.
  • Seine cruise: Bateaux Parisiens 1-hour sightseeing cruise. Departure times change by season, so check Thursday's times and aim for about 16:30–17:00.

Eiffel Tower photo spots

  • Trocadéro terrace: the classic full-tower shot. Go to the left or right edge of the terrace for fewer people in the frame.
  • Pont de Bir-Hakeim: the tower framed by iron arches, with the line 6 train passing overhead.
  • From the boat: the cruise ends below the tower around dusk, when the golden lights come on. The tower sparkles for 5 minutes on the hour after dark, so the first sparkle should be at 18:00.

A story thread for the kids

Napoleon ties the two days together. Thursday: he crowned himself emperor in Notre-Dame and left his hat at Le Procope. Friday: you'll see the giant painting of that coronation at the Louvre, walk where his palace stood in the Tuileries (the Mona Lisa hung in his bedroom), and visit his generals' graves at Père Lachaise, the cemetery he opened. Tell the 4½-year-old to look out for "the little emperor with the big hat".
